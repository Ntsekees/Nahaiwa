# Syntax  
  
The language's syntax is highly head-initial, with some exceptions.  
A sentence always begins with a root verb, and there can be no more than one root verb per sentence. It is formally recognizable by bearing an Illocution role prefix; this doubles as an indicator of the beginning of a new sentence.  
Fragment sentences, not containing any root verb, are not allowed in the language. It is mandatory to provide at least a dummy verb of some sort. When the speaker wishes not to put at the very beginning of the sentence the salient semantic content that should normally be expressed by the root verb, a dummy auxiliary-like root verb is used, and the semantic content whose appearance is to be delayed is instead demoted to a noun phrase governed by the dummy root verb. This allows word order flexibility while retaining the advantages of a rigidly verb-initial syntax.  
  
The root verb is the only true verb in the sentence; subordinate clauses are headed by a participle, which, from the point of view of the inside of the clause, acts as its verb; but from the outside of the subordinate clause, the participle looks like a noun or a word of another part of speech such as an adjective or an adverb, by taking the role prefix corresponding to that part of speech.  
  
## Anaphora

Nahaıwa has a variety of different anaphoric devices, allowing refering back to a previously mentioned referent.

One such device, already mentioned in [the Proforms section](morphology/proforms.md), is the set of case anaphora extentional proforms, such as ⟪-sc◈-⟫ which refers back to the most recent C-commanding noun sporting the Ergative outer case:

  * **Ohaʎáoko cawíƛʰï kuscıʎún.**  
  `o꞊      ha- ʎáoko       c-  a-   wíƛʰï`  
  `ASR;REP꞊NEG-takes_care  ERG-NTRᵢ-is_a_fly`  
  `k-  u-   sc-     ı-  -ʎún`  
  `ACC-ACCᵢ-ERG_ANA-ERGᵢ-is_a_parent_of`  
  *A fly does not take care of its offsprings.*  

Another anaphoric device is that of *named variables*, which are stems marked with the special extensional prefix ⟪-s◈-⟫, immediately preceding a stem that represents the name of a previously bound variable, implicitly bound by a previous noun, and sharing the same stem or root as the noun.

For example:
  * **Acál caθaqánwe kasaqánwe.**  
  `a꞊        cál  c-  a-   θ-  a-  -qánwe`  
  `ASR;INFER꞊see  ERG-NTRᵢ-EXO-NTRᵢ-is_a_human`  
  `k-  a-   s-         a-  -qánwe`  
  `ACC-NTRᵢ-NAMED_VAR-ERGᵢ-is_a_human`  
  *That human sees the aforementioned human.*  
  *That human sees themself.*

## Shadowing of bound variables

Any ‘bound variables’, such as anaphoric proforms or the aforementioned named variables, may be subject to *shadowing* when they are bound again subsequently, which means that the new binding replaces the older one (as long as the new binding's scope remains valid), and the older value of the variable is no longer accessible at all.

For example, if you have a proform ⟪-sc◈-⟫ bound to a previous Ergative-cased noun, but a new noun sporting the Ergative case appears, this proform will get reassigned to that new noun, and the previous value of that proform gets *shadowed*, making it no longer accessible.

One useful trick when one still needs to refer back to the noun whose proform assignment is about to get shadowed, is to re-bind it to a named variable, for example:

* **(……) taınınčáwa ƛatayá takóyu laıtuʎáoko casayá**  
  `t-  aı- nı-  -nčáwa            ƛa-  ta     -yá     t-  a-  -kóyu`  
  `NTR-REL-1:ERG-acquainted_with  PEND-RSM:NTR-thing  NTR-NTRᵢ-dog`  
  `l-   aı- tu-     -ʎáoko         c-  a-   sa-      -yá`  
  `ATTR-REL-RSM:ACCᵢ-take_care_of  ERG-NTRᵢ-NAMED_VAR-thing`  
  *(……) one such that I am familiar, with respect to __the them-thing__, with the dog(s) that __the aforementioned thing__ takes care of.*

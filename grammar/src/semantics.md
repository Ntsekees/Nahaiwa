# Semantics

Nahaıwa nominals and proforms may have one or several simultaneous referents; there is no mandatory indication of the cardinality of a referent set, e.g. whether it is singular or plural.

Nahaıwa nominals and plural do not necessarily refer to concrete extensional individuals; they may very well refer to intensions, kinds and abstractions. As a result, when doing certain things such as counting entities, it is important to explicitly specify that we are counting concrete individuals, e.g. by using the extensional prefix ⟪-mp◈-⟫, which restricts the referents of an argument slot to concrete individuals only.

Nouns without explicit quantifiers in the Determinacy slot do not exhibit implicit quantification; they refer to abstract kinds, and may appear to ‘slip below’ the semantic scope of extensional prefixes sported by the matrix verb. For example:

***“Inıhacál kakóyu.”***  
*“I don't see a dog.”*

While the negator extension ⟪-ha-⟫ has its scope constrained to the verb stem, “to not see”, no existential quantification of the dog takes place in a way as to scope over the negation; the noun ⟪kakóyu⟫ only refer to dogness, and it is ‘instantiated’ as a concrete dog only in the lexical meaning of the root ⟪-cál⟫. From the point of view of traditional extensional logic, this might give off the impression that the negation has scope over the nominal reference “dog”, but this isn't exactly what's going on under the hood.
